## c) Best-price selection bias: confirmed, and it is the one real finding

- The two sides of a match each take the Shin probability of the book that quotes them the longest price. Averaged over matches the two market terms sum to **0.952**,
  not 1.000 (consensus mean: 1.000). The production blend therefore under-states P on every bet side by about 2-3 points, the 2026 refit absorbs that in c = +0.067,
  and on the sides we bet the production P is too low: EV>5% sides mean P 57.8% vs won 61.1% (gap +3.4 pt, se 1.1).
- With the consensus (mean over the books quoting the match, bets still at the best price) the refit intercept is 0.000 (0.012) and the weights are 0.488 (0.036) / 0.608 (0.038),
  i.e. close to the old 0.44 / 0.64 (the fixed old weights are inside ~1.3 se). Fixed 0.44 / 0.64 on the consensus scores best of the non-fitted choices, and
  refitting adds nothing (it is noisier; -0.15 (0.25) vs -0.83 (0.11) for the fixed weights).
- Log-loss -0.83e-3 (0.11), 7 se paired, negative in both halves (-1.01, -0.68), all 4 blocks (-0.58...-1.12) and all 7 walk months (-0.49...-1.37).
  It is small in absolute terms (0.14% of the log-loss), but the blend beats the market alone by only 0.0045, so it is about a fifth of the blend's gain over the market.
- Bet-side calibration (pooled, mean P vs won share):

- **ROI is where it needs care.** With the same EV>5% rule the consensus P is about 5 EV points higher on the typical bet, so the rule fires 2.7x as often:
  5,068 bets, ROI +11.1% (1.5), lower 90% +8.6%, vs 1,869 bets, +17.2% (2.4), +13.2%. Total profit rises by +240 units (se 57) because the extra bets still earn about +7% each,

## d) Platt / isotonic / odds-dependent shrink

- Isotonic on top of the blend: worse log-loss (+1.1 to +1.2e-3, 2.3 se) and worse ROI at equal N (-4.4 (2.2) points). Do not use.
- Platt on top of the production blend: -0.24 (0.12)e-3 (2 se), it fits a positive shift (the same 0.95 artefact as c), picks 4,439 bets at EV>5%, ROI +11.8%, same-N ROI +18.4% (+1.2 (1.3) vs prod). It is a cruder
  version of the consensus fix (consensus is better: -0.83 (0.11)). Platt on the refit changes nothing (the refit already contains an intercept).
- Odds-dependent weights (log odds interaction or bands 2 / 3.5): no gain (+0.07 (0.24), +0.29 (0.26)e-3), the refit weights barely depend on the odds. "More market weight at longer odds" is not supported.
  Combined with the consensus (log-odds interaction): -0.13 (0.24), no better than the plain consensus.

## Recommendation

1. **Keep the coefficients 0.44 / 0.64 / c=0.** No refit (all, per tour, per level group, odds-dependent) beat them out-of-sample on log-loss, group-specific fits are significantly worse, isotonic is worse.
2. The current blend is **under-confident on the sides we bet** (EV>5%: P 57.8% vs won 61.1%) because the market term takes the Shin probability of the best-price book, and the two best prices sum to 0.95 (so the term is
   biased low on average). **Replacing the market term by the mean of the per-book Shin probabilities of the books quoting the match (still betting the best price) wins on log-loss (-0.83e-3, 7 se, all splits)
   and calibrates the bet sides (gap +0.3 (0.7) pt).**
3. That change does **not** improve ROI. With the unchanged EV>5% rule it produces 2.7x the bets at +11.1% (lower 90% +8.6%) instead of +17.2% (+13.2%); only with the threshold raised to about EV>10% (same number of bets) is the ROI the
   same (+17.5% vs +17.2%, difference within 1 se). By your criterion (wins on log-loss and does not reduce ROI on the test block) it passes only as the pair **consensus market term + EV>10%**, and then the gain is calibration (matters for Kelly sizing and for reported
   probabilities), not profit. If staying with flat stakes and the EV>5% rule, leave production as it is. Do not switch the threshold and the market term separately.
4. Before changing live code: paper_trade.py would need all books' opening prices of the match at bet time (the six-book Shin mean is computed from the same odds file); log both P versions in the ledger for a forward test of the consensus
   (n around 1,000 bets at EV>10% gives about 3% ROI se, so ROI cannot be separated from production; only calibration can).
5. Nothing in this study was fitted on 2026 outcomes other than the refit variants; the consensus winner uses the old fixed coefficients, but the choice of this variant out of ~40 tried is itself a (mild) selection, which is why the
   statement is limited to "log-loss and calibration better, ROI unchanged".
